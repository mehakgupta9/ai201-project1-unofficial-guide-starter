Reply by nillawiffer (6 upvotes):
(In full disclosure, I am surprised you were able to get permission for this class.)

Probably sizing up this course description using a typical undergrad perspective (“gee, how many tests or projects?”) will only tell part of the story of what is in store for you. Grad school (especially in CS) is an entirely different culture.

Do a couple of the papers on the reading list each take a solid week or two to get your head around? Yeah, suck it up. Nobody cares.

Is the professor free to leverage ambiguity in the syllabus to fill in what they want as they go? Yup. Deal with it.

Will anyone hold you by the hand, lead you along the way, and remind you of obligations? Not a chance.

It is what it is, and what it is not is like any undergrad course you have ever experienced.

Some 600-level courses might look like a “stout” 400-level course. Those can be a gentler introduction to graduate school expectations.

Reply by nillawiffer (1 upvote):
I don’t know that anyone thinks you are lying.

What seems surprising is that the undergrad director approved it.

The overwhelming advising lore would predict a poor outcome in short order. A student actually prepared for upper-level work is someone they’d already know about and be working with separately.

The CMSC848 course won’t count toward major requirements, and the opportunity cost is a 400-level class that could bring someone closer to graduation.

The seminar looks like it is being taught by a brand-new assistant professor who has never taught here before. They likely have no clue about advising practices and are delighted to have a student—any student—ask about joining a low-demand seminar.

The honest message from a knowledgeable professor is often “it’s no problem,” which in graduate school means “…for them.”

Undergraduate advisors are supposed to patiently guide students toward things that are useful for them. In this case, they gave a rubber-stamp approval. Hope it works out for you!
